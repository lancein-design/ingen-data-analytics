Week 2 Log — Competitive Market Intelligence and KSCP Financial Analysis
Yincheng Zhang 

This week’s most important lesson was that revenue growth alone is insufficient evidence of successful robotics commercialization. Across the Market Intelligence Report, Excel market dataset, and KSCP analysis notebook, my central analytical decision was to examine what drove the reported growth and whether it translated into sustainable economics.

Knightscope provided a useful public financial comparable for Aido Rover and Sentinel Prime AI. My six-quarter analysis showed that Q2 2026 revenue reached approximately $9.0 million, up 228.3% year over year, while gross margin improved to 7.4%. However, the report’s segment analysis showed that Security Force contributed approximately $6.83 million of quarterly revenue, compared with approximately $0.92 million from autonomous security robots. I therefore treated the consolidated increase as partly acquisition-driven and avoided interpreting it as evidence of equivalent growth in robot deployment or demand.

The expense and liquidity analysis reinforced this distinction. FY2025 R&D and SG&A together represented approximately 256.8% of revenue. Using June 2026 cash and average operating cash burn over the latest four quarters, I estimated a runway of approximately 0.8 quarters. This was a historical scenario estimate, not a prediction of when the company would exhaust its cash. It highlighted why commercialization should be assessed through margins, service costs, and financing requirements alongside revenue.

Another important decision concerned missing and noncomparable data. I retained unavailable company figures as N/A and did not calculate revenue per deployed robot because Knightscope’s broader network count includes devices and security personnel. I also kept SG&A combined because the prepared dataset did not support a separate breakdown. For cash flow, I distinguished cumulative reporting from quarterly activity, deriving Q2 2026 operating cash burn as $23.099 million minus $11.604 million, or $11.495 million. In Python, I used pandas to reshape the workbook, documented formulas, and added checks for gross-margin reconciliation and source completeness.

The market-sizing exercise required similar caution. Service robotics and security robotics overlap, and both include applications outside InGen’s immediate target market. I therefore used headline estimates to describe market direction rather than adding them together or treating them as InGen’s addressable market.

For InGen, the main implication is to establish measurable deployment economics before interpreting scale as commercial success. Aido Rover and Sentinel Prime AI should track robot-level revenue, direct service costs, uptime, maintenance, and customer retention separately from broader service revenue. These are proposed measurement priorities, not conclusions about InGen’s actual performance. In Week 3, I will translate them into explicit KPI definitions and data requirements, using clearly labeled synthetic data to demonstrate the pipeline where internal operational records remain unavailable.
