Week 5 Weekly Log: Text Collection and NLP Analysis

This week, I developed an exploratory NLP pipeline to examine how collected sources describe eldercare robotics, including ElliQ, Fari, and robotic pets. The final corpus contains 20 documents from eight publishers across four source categories: company content, press coverage, government programs, and editorial review. I documented the collection procedures, inclusion criteria, source metadata, and limitations in the text data collection document.

Before modeling, I reviewed the extracted text and applied targeted corrections, including repairing encoding problems, recovering a missing review conclusion, and removing identified promotional content and repeated paragraphs. I preserved the reviewed text separately from the modeling input. Preprocessing used NLTK tokenization, lowercasing, custom stopword removal, POS-aware lemmatization, and a three-character minimum token length. Validation confirmed 11,683 retained tokens and a vocabulary of 2,441 unique terms.

For TF-IDF analysis, I identified the top 20 terms across the corpus and the top 10 terms within each source category. Company content emphasized care, family, and daily support, while government documents emphasized programs and partnerships. Company content and press coverage had the highest cross-category cosine similarity, at 0.4613. These similarities indicate vocabulary overlap rather than agreement or shared sentiment. An unexpected result was that “not” ranked highest overall. This reflects the deliberate retention of negation during preprocessing and does not, by itself, indicate negative sentiment.

For LDA, I compared models with three to eight topics using training perplexity and qualitative inspection. Although the six-topic model achieved the lowest training perplexity, I selected five topics for interpretability after reviewing keywords and representative article excerpts. The topics were Social Companionship, Product Features and Use, Caregiver and Family Support, Fari Technical Design, and Robotic Pets and Wellbeing. A document-topic heatmap showed that Caregiver and Family Support dominated 12 of the 20 documents.

The most surprising topic was Fari Technical Design, which formed a distinct topic dominated by a single document. This demonstrated how specialized technical vocabulary can separate one article from the rest of a small corpus. It also highlighted the need to interpret model outputs cautiously: a distinct topic does not establish a widespread market concern.

The main limitation is the small, uneven sample. Company content represents 55% of the documents, and only one editorial review is included. No formal coherence score or held-out perplexity was calculated. The findings therefore describe the collected sources rather than customer preferences or broader public opinion.
